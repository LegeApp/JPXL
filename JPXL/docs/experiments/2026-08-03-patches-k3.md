# Patches (K.3): what the corpus streams contain, and how the path was proved

Date: 2026-08-03. Status: frozen.

## Why this note exists

`bike`, `bike_5`, `progressive` and `progressive_5` are the conformance
corpus's colour VarDCT cases, and all four were refused at
`frame_header.flags.patches`. Implementing K.3 is what unblocks them — but the
one case that would grade it end to end, `bike_5`, is *also* blocked by the
open modular bug, so the usual "the corpus number moved" evidence was not
available. This note records what was done instead.

## What the streams actually contain

Read with JPXL's own header parser (no oracle emits this per-frame):

| case | frames |
| --- | --- |
| `bike`, `bike_5` | `kReferenceOnly` **kModular**, cropped 22x20 at (0,0), `save_before_ct`, slot 0 → `kRegularFrame` **kVarDCT** 2048x2560, `patches`, EPF 3, gaborish |
| `progressive`, `progressive_5` | the same 29x28 reference frame → **`kLFFrame`** 4064x2704 → `kRegularFrame` kVarDCT with `patches`, `kUseLfFrame`, **2 passes**, `want_icc` |

So patches alone unblock `bike`/`bike_5`. `progressive` additionally needs
`kLFFrame` storage and the G.2.2 `kUseLfFrame` path plus multi-pass HF decode;
both are refused with typed `Unsupported` naming F.2 and G.2.2.

`bike_5`'s dictionary, decoded: **94 patches, 94 positions, every one from
reference slot 0, every blend mode `kAdd`**, rectangles of 4x4, 2x4, 3x2 and
similar tiling the 22x20 reference. No alpha mode, no `clamp`, no
`alpha_channel` field anywhere.

## How K.3.1 was proved without pixels

K.3.1 reads its dictionary from a complete C.3.3 stream, so it ends in the
C.3.2 terminal ANS state. `SymbolDecoder::finish()` therefore proves the entire
model — the ten context assignments, every `+ 1`, the `UnpackSigned` position
deltas, and the two different field guards (`mode > 3` for `alpha_channel`,
`mode > 2` for `clamp`) — with no reference data. That is the same free gate
that settled the modular and HF-coefficient slices.

Stronger still, and the measurement that made this note worth writing:
**`LfGlobal` of `bike_5`'s VarDCT frame is consumed to the bit.** With the
patch dictionary in place it reads 12293 of the section's 12296 bits, leaving
three bits of byte padding. `LfGlobal` for that frame is, in order, the patch
dictionary (K.3.1), `LfChannelDequantization` (G.1.2), the three `kVarDCT`
bundles (I.2.1–I.2.3) and the global MA tree (G.1.3). Exact exhaustion means
every one of them is parsed correctly, at the correct bit position — including
K.3.1's placement as the *first* row of Table G.1.

Before this slice the same frame failed with `H.2: use_global_tree is set but
no global MA tree was supplied`, hundreds of bytes downstream, because the
unread dictionary shifted everything after it. That is the characteristic shape
of a missing bundle and is worth recognising: the error names the first
structure whose *contents* stop being self-consistent, not the one that is
wrong.

## How K.3.2 was proved end to end

`bike_5` cannot be graded yet, so synthetic streams were used. cjxl emits
patches for repeated content at any size, so a 200x200 greyscale image with
four 8x8 glyphs repeated on a 16-pixel grid produces the same two-frame
structure as `bike_5` — and 200x200 is single-group, which dodges the modular
bug entirely.

Four such streams (`-d 0.5/1.0/2.0`, `-e 7/8/9`), each with 4 patches at 144
positions read from reference slot **3** (so the slot machinery is exercised
at a nonzero index), graded against `djxl --output_format npy`:

| stream | patches rendered | patches skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `-d 1.0 -e 7` | peak **7.6e-4**, RMSE 2.0e-4 | peak 0.502, RMSE 0.240 |
| `-d 2.0 -e 8` | peak **4.5e-3**, RMSE 8.6e-4 | peak 0.505, RMSE 0.239 |
| `-d 0.5 -e 9` | peak **5.5e-4**, RMSE 1.1e-4 | peak 0.502, RMSE 0.240 |

All three land well inside the Part 3 with-filters class (0.06 / 0.02); with
the blit disabled the error is three orders of magnitude worse and the glyphs
are simply absent. The A/B is what makes this evidence rather than a
coincidence: it shows the patches carry the content and that the decoder puts
it in the right place, in the right colour space, with the right blend.

(The fourth stream, `-d 1.0 -e 9`, fails in its own `LfQuant` — the modular
bug again. Its dictionary decodes; only the frame data does not.)

That A/B also exercises the two pieces that have no unit test of their own:
the `kReferenceOnly` frame decode, and L.2.2's `kModular` pre-step that turns
its integer samples into the XYB a patch is blended in.

## The channel-order trap in the reference frame

L.2.2's `kModular` paragraph reads: "the integer sample values y', x', B' (the
values in the first three channels) are first converted to X, Y, B samples as
follows: `X = x' * m_x_lf_unscaled`, `Y = y' * m_y_lf_unscaled`,
`B = (B' + y') * m_b_lf_unscaled`."

Channel **0 is y', channel 1 is x'** — the same Y, X, B ordering that G.2.2's
`LfQuant` turned out to use and that wave 2 had to reverse a flip point for.
Reading them as X, Y, B swaps the two chroma-carrying planes of every patch.
The synthetic A/B above catches it: the glyph content is achromatic, so a swap
leaves the luma roughly right and the chroma badly wrong, which shows up as a
peak error near 0.1 rather than 5e-4.

`m_*_lf_unscaled` comes from the **reference frame's own** G.1.2 bundle, not
the VarDCT frame's — each frame carries its own, and they need not agree.

## Pipeline position

Clause 4 states the order plainly: frame data (Annex G), restoration filters
(Annex J), image features (Annex K), "finally ... colour transforms as
specified in Annex L". K.3.2 says the same thing twice from its own side —
"after applying restoration filters (Annex J)" and "the sample values
`new_sample` are in the colour space before the inverse colour transforms from
L.2, L.3 and L.4 are applied". So for a `kVarDCT` frame the blit happens on the
XYB planes `epf` just produced, between `apply_restoration` and
`to_linear_srgb`. No ambiguity, no flip point.

## Open flip points

Two, both unexercised, both marked in the source:

* `PATCH_REFERENCE_IS_CANVAS_COORDINATES` (`decode.rs`) — whether
  `Reference[ref]`'s coordinates are canvas coordinates or are relative to the
  reference frame's own crop origin. Every reference frame in the corpus has
  crop origin `(0, 0)`, where the readings coincide exactly. The canvas reading
  ships, because "Reference" means a canvas-composited thing everywhere else in
  F.2.
* `ALPHA_GUARD_COUNTS_EXTRA_CHANNELS` (`frame/patches.rs`) — whether K.3.1's
  "there is more than 1 alpha channel" counts extra channels or only
  `kAlpha`-typed ones. It changes the *bit count* of a dictionary for an image
  with two or more extra channels, so a wrong answer desynchronises the rest of
  it. Unreachable today: patches on a frame with extra channels are refused.

## What is refused, and where

| construct | clause cited |
| --- | --- |
| patches on a `kModular` frame | K.3 (also a bit-position guard: the modular path does not read the dictionary) |
| patches with extra channels | K.3.2 |
| an alpha-using blend mode (Table K.1 rows 4–7) | K.3.2 |
| a `kVarDCT` reference frame | F.2 |
| a reference frame in a non-XYB image | L.2.2 |
| a greyscale reference frame in an XYB image | L.2.2 |
| `kLFFrame` / `kSkipProgressive` frames | F.2 |

Nothing in that list is reachable by any stream in the corpus or in
`tests/fixtures/`, except `kLFFrame`, which is what `progressive` needs next.

## Addendum, 2026-08-04 — the two open flip points, re-probed with extra channels live

Wave 6 removed the "patches with extra channels" refusal (K.3.2 now blends
every channel group, and `apply_patches` takes the frame's extra planes), so
the `patches` and `patches_5` corpus cases decode and grade. That makes the two
flip points above reachable for the first time. Both were re-probed against
every stream now available. **Neither is settled; both stay where they were.**

* `PATCH_REFERENCE_IS_CANVAS_COORDINATES` — still unexercised. The
  `patches`/`patches_lossless` reference frames are cropped 207x189 and 198x198
  rectangles but both sit at crop origin `(0, 0)`, exactly like `bike`'s and
  `progressive`'s, so the two readings coincide sample for sample. No stream in
  the corpus carries a reference frame at a non-zero origin.
* `ALPHA_GUARD_COUNTS_EXTRA_CHANNELS` — reachable but still not
  discriminating. The guard only bites when `num_extra >= 2` with fewer than
  two of them `kAlpha`. Every corpus case that combines patches with extra
  channels (`patches`, `patches_lossless`) has exactly **one** extra channel,
  where "more than 1" is false under both readings and no `alpha_channel` field
  is present at all. The multi-extra-channel cases (`spot` with three,
  `cmyk_layers` with two) carry no patch dictionary.

What `patches`'s dictionary does contain, decoded by JPXL and counted: **139
patches, 654 positions**, and per position exactly two blend rules — the colour
group at `kAdd` (654 of them) and the alpha group at `kNone` (654). So the case
proves the *bit layout* of a `num_extra + 1`-long rule list (any miscount
desynchronises the C.3.2 stream and `SymbolDecoder::finish()` fails), and it
proves that a `kNone` alpha rule leaves the alpha plane untouched — the graded
alpha channel comes back with peak error 0.0. It does not reach any
alpha-weighted patch mode, which is why the new
`PATCH_ALPHA_IS_THE_PATCHS_OWN` flip point (see the 2026-08-04 report) is
recorded rather than proved.
